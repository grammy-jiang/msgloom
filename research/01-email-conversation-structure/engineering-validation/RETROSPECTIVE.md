# Topic 01 engineering-validation retrospective

## What worked

- Production data was not required to make progress. Deterministic synthetic/adversarial fixtures exposed concrete structural failures with exact ground truth.
- Separating quote detection, source matching, parent relations, ancestry, and provenance direction prevented adjacent metrics from being mistaken for stronger capabilities.
- The unsafe-vs-ambiguity-preserving duplicate Message-ID comparator produced a concrete, reproducible reason not to use a one-value identifier reduction.
- A strict arrival-time E2 path exposed errors hidden by gold-conditioned/final-context diagnostics.
- Independent review materially improved the research instead of serving as a formality.

## Review-driven corrections

### Review 1 — rejected

The first engineering result was not accepted because:

- fixture expected text was generated by the extractor under test, making extraction accuracy circular;
- accepted source attributions attached to false-positive quote detections were absent from the end-to-end error denominator.

The same review also identified E4 split overlap, replay scoring blind spots, composite-vs-alternative source semantics, source-availability leakage, and missing validity regressions.

### Review 2 — accepted with issues

The major repairs were independently confirmed. Remaining issues were regression protection for one-to-one matching, a stale E4 narrative sentence, and ambiguity between experiment-execution and review-acceptance status.

### Review 3 — accepted

All issues were fixed. The reviewer independently reproduced current artifacts, ran 55 tests, and used targeted mutations to verify the new regressions fail when the guarded behavior is broken.

## Process lessons for later topics

1. Synthetic gold must be authored independently of the component being evaluated.
2. End-to-end metrics must include false-positive predictions, not only predictions aligned to gold items.
3. Arrival-time evaluation must not leak future evidence; full-context diagnostics must be labeled separately.
4. One-to-one matching needs mutation-level tests, not only normal examples.
5. Machine-readable experiment execution status and independent acceptance status should be separate concepts.
6. Independent reviewer rejection should be treated as a gate and used to improve methodology, not worked around by softer wording.
7. Preserve failed/rejected review artifacts as research history.
