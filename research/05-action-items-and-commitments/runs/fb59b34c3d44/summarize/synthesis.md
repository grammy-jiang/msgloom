# Executive Summary

This corpus of 29 studies provides direct workplace-email and chat evidence for detecting requests, commitments, and some responsibility relations, plus meeting and adjacent-domain evidence for structured action components, temporal relations, modality, negation, and event arguments. The strongest direct evidence supports contextual interpretation, sentence- or clause-level localization, multi-label act phenomena, and explicit owner-assignment tasks. However, no study jointly validates speech-act type, owner, action object, deadline binding, conditions or exceptions, and exact provenance in authentic modern workplace email or chat. Deadline binding and conditional or negated obligation attachment remain especially under-tested. Evidence-span supervision exists for some email intents and generic event extraction, while implicit and scattered arguments challenge simple contiguous-span assumptions. Decision extraction is better studied in meetings than authentic workplace messaging. The nine carried-over gaps therefore remain open; the engineering gaps require benchmark work rather than further literature alone.

# Themes

The first recurring theme is that **workplace request, commitment, and action-item detection is empirically feasible, but structured argument extraction is much less established**. Direct email studies detect Promise Action, requests, commitments, and action-item-bearing sentences with useful but imperfect performance, yet their principal outputs are usually message- or sentence-level labels rather than complete owner-action-deadline-condition records [2005.13084] [doi-10-1145-3289600-3290984] [doi-10-1145-3331184-3331260] [doi-10-1145-1076034-1076140].

A second theme is that **structured decomposition has empirical support in meeting action-item work, but the individual arguments are not equally tractable**. Meeting studies decompose action items into Description, Owner, Timeframe, and Agreement and obtain better region-level detection than flat formulations in some experiments. Owner was the weakest component in the early structured experiments, with substantially lower F1 than Agreement [doi-10-1007-11965152-18] [doi-10-3115-1564535-1564540] [doi-10-18653-v1-2007-sigdial-1-4].

Direct email evidence makes the owner problem more concrete. The EPA dataset treats responsibility as potentially implicit, plural, or absent and allows multiple responsible people and a no-one outcome rather than assuming one assignee. Its Enron owner labels nevertheless show only moderate agreement, and transfer from Avocado to Enron is imperfect [doi-10-18653-v1-w18-6104]. The commitment-monitoring study separately maps task performer and beneficiary to debtor and creditor roles, giving additional direct email/chat evidence that responsibility is representable as a structured relation rather than only an intent label [doi-10-1109-scc-2013-62].

A third theme is that **context frequently matters, but more context is not universally better**. In Avocado, full-message context increased human recall for Request Information and improved the best context-sensitive models [doi-10-1145-3331184-3331260]. Longer context also improved dialogue-act classification in one workplace Slack team [2312.05471], and Context-Drop improved meeting action-item detection [2303.16763]. However, collective email classification degraded some delivery-related acts, and the best Context-Drop configuration differed between AMC-A and AMI [doi-10-1145-1076034-1076094] [2303.16763].

A fourth theme is that **conditionality and indirectness are persistent direct-workplace ambiguities**. In Enron annotation, conditional offers accounted for a large share of commitment disagreements, implicit requests often needed information elsewhere in the message, and missing organizational relationship information affected interpretation [manual-0839b98ee7]. Revised guidance that handled conditional and indirect cases more explicitly raised commitment agreement substantially [manual-d19b0f6f1e] [manual-20b8cd2fdd]. The commitment-monitoring model formally permits conditional antecedents, but its operational empirical treatment concentrates mainly on unconditional commitments [doi-10-1109-scc-2013-62].

A related theme is that **negation and modality require scope resolution rather than cue lookup alone**. BioScope explicitly binds negation and speculation cues to scopes, while the *SEM shared task shows a large gap between cue detection and complete negation resolution [doi-10-1186-1471-2105-9-s11-s9] [manual-ee40d588e3]. Event-temporal extraction also identifies negation as a prominent error source [1909.10094], and modality work distinguishes triggers from their semantic targets [1410.4868]. These are adjacent-domain results; they do not establish workplace obligation extraction accuracy.

For temporal information, the corpus supports **time-expression and temporal-relation modeling more strongly than workplace deadline binding**. Meeting work annotates Timeframe and obtains useful timeframe-summary performance [doi-10-18653-v1-2007-sigdial-1-4]. TimeML provides EVENT, TIMEX3, and TLINK structures for event-time relations in general text [manual-1e8332286e]. In contrast, the EPA work leaves temporal assignment to future work, the Enron request/commitment representation explicitly leaves deadlines as an extension, and commitment monitoring uses deadline presence as a feature rather than evaluating an extracted deadline-to-consequent relation [doi-10-18653-v1-w18-6104] [manual-d19b0f6f1e] [doi-10-1109-scc-2013-62].

The evidence-provenance literature exposes another important distinction. Enterprise-email intent annotation includes highlighted manifestation spans [doi-10-1145-3331184-3331260], and PAIE demonstrates explicit span-role extraction on general event benchmarks [2202.12109]. DiscourseEE, however, finds that many arguments in its Reddit health domain are implicit or scattered, meaning that a semantically required argument may have no single contiguous source span [2410.03594]. These papers validate separate aspects; they do not jointly validate a combined workplace provenance scheme.

The corpus also demonstrates **multi-act and multi-action phenomena**. Avocado messages often carry more than one annotated intent [doi-10-1145-3331184-3331260]. Lin handles coordinated verbs as separate tasks [doi-10-18653-v1-2020-coling-main-164]. The Enron edge-case analysis explicitly identifies cases that simultaneously function as commitments and requests [manual-20b8cd2fdd], while collective email-act classification permits multiple message-level labels [doi-10-1145-1076034-1076094].

Finally, **domain and preprocessing choices materially affect reported performance**. Commitment detection degrades when models transfer between Enron and Avocado without adaptation [doi-10-1145-3289600-3290984]. Removing quoted, forwarded, signature, and other non-author zones substantially improves Enron request classification [manual-cd3fa58e4c]. Separate manual annotation work reports a non-trivial sentence-segmentation error rate [manual-0839b98ee7]. These findings demonstrate sensitivity to corpus and preprocessing conditions rather than a corpus-independent extraction result.

Structured decision evidence is comparatively indirect. Scenario-driven AMI meeting work successfully models Issue, Resolution Proposal, Resolution Restatement, and Agreement [doi-10-3115-1708376-1708410]. The workplace Slack taxonomy distinguishes proposals, acceptance, rejection, task assignment, and voluntary ownership [2312.05471]. Neither study demonstrates full structured decision arguments in authentic workplace email or enterprise chat.

# Contradictions

**Context benefit is not uniform.** Avocado intent classification, workplace Slack classification, and meeting action-item detection report improvements from contextual modeling [doi-10-1145-3331184-3331260] [2312.05471] [2303.16763]. In contrast, collective email classification degraded Deliver and deliveredData in some settings, and the Context-Drop configuration that was strongest on AMC-A was not strongest on AMI [doi-10-1145-1076034-1076094] [2303.16763]. The studies therefore support task- and corpus-specific context gains, not a universal claim that expanding context always improves extraction.

**Sentence-level and message-level granularity produce different advantages.** Sentence-level training improved document action-item detection relative to document-trained classifiers in one email experiment [doi-10-1145-1076034-1076140]. By contrast, the Enron annotation studies obtained higher request and commitment agreement at message level, partly because message annotation avoids ambiguity over which individual sentence is the locus of an act [manual-20b8cd2fdd]. These results evaluate different outcomes and cannot be silently averaged into one preferred granularity.

**Questions are operationalized differently across task-extraction schemes.** Lin assumes questions are not tasks and therefore excludes them from positive task extraction [doi-10-18653-v1-2020-coling-main-164]. The Enron request framework explicitly defines questions requiring an informational response as requests because they impose an actionable obligation [manual-d19b0f6f1e]. The HICSS email taxonomy likewise distinguishes information requests from directives before merging them for experimental sparsity [doi-10-1109-hicss-2006-528].

**Negated action language receives incompatible operational interpretations across systems.** Lin filters negated imperatives from positive tasks [doi-10-18653-v1-2020-coling-main-164], while commitment monitoring interprets certain negated matching actions as cancellation of a previously established commitment [doi-10-1109-scc-2013-62]. BioScope and the *SEM shared task instead separate negation cue and scope without assigning workplace-action semantics [doi-10-1186-1471-2105-9-s11-s9] [manual-ee40d588e3]. These treatments cannot be collapsed into one undifferentiated negative-action label.

**The definition of an action item differs materially across studies.** The structured meeting formulation requires a concrete future action, responsible person, agreement, and reasonably specific timeframe [doi-10-1007-11965152-18] [doi-10-3115-1564535-1564540]. The email action-item study uses a broader formulation centered on the sender intending to elicit an action or response [doi-10-1145-1076034-1076140]. Their reported metrics therefore concern different target concepts.

**Speech-act exclusivity is also inconsistent.** The HICSS experiment reduces its taxonomy to five classifier classes, merging some theoretically distinct acts [doi-10-1109-hicss-2006-528]. The Enron edge-case study explicitly finds utterances that function simultaneously as commitments and requests [manual-20b8cd2fdd], while the collective-classification study uses multi-label email acts [doi-10-1145-1076034-1076094].

# Open Gaps

**gap-1 — ACADEMIC, HIGH — OPEN.** There is still no direct modern workplace email/chat benchmark in this corpus that jointly annotates speech-act type, owner or assignee, action object, deadline, conditions or exceptions, and exact evidence spans. Existing papers separately cover owner assignment, email intent spans, meeting action-item components, or general event arguments, but not the complete joint target [doi-10-18653-v1-w18-6104] [doi-10-1145-3331184-3331260] [doi-10-1007-11965152-18] [2202.12109]. Suggested query: `"action item extraction" workplace email chat owner assignee deadline condition span corpus`.

**gap-2 — ACADEMIC, HIGH — OPEN.** Direct empirical evidence for extracting decisions, approvals, rejections, and proposals with structured arguments from authentic workplace email or chat is still missing. AMI provides structured decision-dialogue evidence but is scenario-driven meeting data, while the Slack study provides act labels without a complete decision-argument structure [doi-10-3115-1708376-1708410] [2312.05471]. Suggested query: `"decision extraction" workplace email approval rejection proposal structured argument dialogue act`.

**gap-3 — ACADEMIC, HIGH — OPEN.** EPA significantly strengthens the evidence base for owner assignment because it directly evaluates email assignees and permits multiple owners and no-one outcomes, but implicit ownership remains noisy, candidate owners are constrained, and the corpus does not close group ownership, multiple actions with different owners, or organization-dependent owner recovery [doi-10-18653-v1-w18-6104] [2312.05471] [doi-10-1007-11965152-18]. Suggested query: `("assignee extraction" OR "owner extraction" OR "responsibility extraction") email action item implicit argument workplace`.

**gap-4 — ACADEMIC, HIGH — OPEN.** The corpus still does not establish reliable workplace deadline-to-action binding. Meeting timeframe extraction and TimeML event-time relations are relevant partial evidence, but distinguishing a true deadline for one action from a mentioned date, scheduling expression, shared date, or conditional future time remains unevaluated in direct workplace structured extraction [doi-10-18653-v1-2007-sigdial-1-4] [manual-1e8332286e] [doi-10-18653-v1-w18-6104] [manual-d19b0f6f1e]. Suggested query: `"deadline extraction" email action item temporal relation due date event argument workplace`.

**gap-5 — ACADEMIC, HIGH — OPEN.** Direct workplace evidence remains insufficient for attaching conditions, exceptions, negation, and modality to the correct action when several actions or clauses coexist. Enron studies establish conditionality as an annotation difficulty, while modality and negation-scope studies supply transfer evidence rather than workplace multi-action validation [manual-0839b98ee7] [manual-d19b0f6f1e] [manual-20b8cd2fdd] [1410.4868] [manual-ee40d588e3]. Suggested query: `("conditional commitment" OR "deontic modality") workplace email negation scope condition action extraction`.

**gap-6 — ENGINEERING, HIGH — OPEN.** Build an independent argument-level benchmark and adversarial fixture suite covering implicit owners, multiple actions in one sentence, shared deadlines, conditional and negated actions, overlapping request/commitment labels, quoted-history contamination, and attachment references; score false positives, false negatives, and each argument independently. The literature supplies examples of these individual phenomena but does not constitute that project benchmark [doi-10-18653-v1-w18-6104] [manual-20b8cd2fdd] [2410.03594].

**gap-7 — ENGINEERING, HIGH — OPEN.** Compare sentence-local, message-level, and selectively expanded thread-context extraction under controlled ablations. The literature contains both context gains and context-induced or act-specific degradation, but the requested controlled comparison has not been performed for this extraction setting [doi-10-1145-3331184-3331260] [2312.05471] [2303.16763] [doi-10-1145-1076034-1076094].

**gap-8 — ENGINEERING, HIGH — OPEN.** Test author, quoted, and forwarded-content zoning while preserving provenance and determine whether zoning improves act and argument extraction without losing obligations that depend on earlier-message context. Zoning improves Enron request classification, while other studies demonstrate that prior context can be necessary; the joint experiment remains undone [manual-cd3fa58e4c] [doi-10-1145-3331184-3331260] [doi-10-18653-v1-w18-6104].

**gap-9 — ENGINEERING, HIGH — OPEN.** Evaluate uncertainty-preserving structured output in which owner, deadline, condition, or act subtype can remain unresolved, measuring false obligations, wrong argument assignments, and recall. Existing work documents ambiguous owners, conditions, scopes, and evidence, but does not perform this comparison [doi-10-18653-v1-w18-6104] [manual-0839b98ee7] [2410.03594] [manual-20b8cd2fdd].

All nine carried-over gaps therefore remain open. None is closed merely because the corpus now contains evidence for one component of the larger problem.

# Actionable Insights

- **Synthesis-derived recommendation:** represent each extracted work item as a typed event whose owner, object, deadline, condition or exception, modality or polarity, and provenance can be populated independently, with unresolved fields remaining explicitly unknown rather than being filled by default.
- **Synthesis-derived recommendation:** allow multiple acts and multiple actions within one message or clause, including overlapping request and commitment interpretations, rather than forcing one mutually exclusive message-level label.
- **Synthesis-derived recommendation:** represent owner assignment as potentially multi-valued and explicitly distinguish stated owners, contextually inferred owners, group ownership, no identified owner, and unresolved ownership.
- **Synthesis-derived recommendation:** model deadlines as relations between temporal expressions and specific extracted actions, retaining original time wording separately from any normalized value.
- **Synthesis-derived recommendation:** represent conditions, prerequisites, exceptions, prohibitions, and negation as scoped relations to affected actions rather than as message-wide flags.
- **Synthesis-derived recommendation:** preserve two provenance modes for arguments: direct source span or spans when textually grounded, and explicitly marked inferred or reconstructed values when the argument is implicit or scattered.
- **Synthesis-derived recommendation:** evaluate sentence-local, message-level, and selective thread-context policies separately instead of adopting maximum context by default; include act-specific false-positive and false-negative analysis.
- **Synthesis-derived recommendation:** zone author text, quoted history, forwarded material, signatures, and other message regions without deleting their lineage, so contextual evidence can still be consulted when interpretation depends on earlier material.
- **Synthesis-derived recommendation:** keep antecedent or proposition scope supplied by Topic 04 distinct from Topic 05 act classification; an extracted approval, rejection, request, or commitment label should not itself be treated as proof that the correct proposition was selected.
- **Synthesis-derived recommendation:** construct evaluation cases that deliberately combine multiple actions, different owners, shared or competing dates, conditional clauses, negation, indirect requests, attachment references, and missing fields, because the existing literature usually tests these phenomena separately rather than as one joint structured task.

# Confidence Summary

Confidence is **high** for the descriptive conclusion that direct workplace-email research supports request and commitment detection, that context can materially affect interpretation, and that owner assignment, conditionality, domain shift, and preprocessing are non-trivial empirical problems [2005.13084] [doi-10-1145-3289600-3290984] [doi-10-18653-v1-w18-6104] [manual-d19b0f6f1e] [manual-cd3fa58e4c].

There are also **well-supported design principles** at the level of argument decomposition, contextual interpretation, and provenance-aware annotation, based on evidence from structured meeting annotation, workplace email and Slack studies, and event-argument research [doi-10-1007-11965152-18] [doi-10-1145-3331184-3331260] [2312.05471] [2202.12109].

Confidence is only **medium** for transferring meeting, biomedical, news, literary, Reddit, or telephone-dialogue findings to modern enterprise email and chat [doi-10-1186-1471-2105-9-s11-s9] [manual-ee40d588e3] [2410.03594] [manual-01c535c108].

No paper in the corpus validates the full combined scheme of act type plus owner, object, deadline relation, condition or exception, polarity, and evidence provenance. Papers that validate individual components therefore do not jointly establish the performance of that combined structured extraction scheme.

# Limitations

- This synthesis is limited to the supplied 29 per-paper analyses; the source papers were not independently re-read during this synthesis step.
- The evidence base mixes authentic workplace email, one-team workplace Slack, simulated or scenario-driven meetings, task-oriented chat, telephone dialogue, biomedical text, literary text, news-oriented temporal corpora, and Reddit health discourse. Transfer-domain results cannot establish production workplace accuracy [2312.05471] [doi-10-3115-1708376-1708410] [doi-10-1186-1471-2105-9-s11-s9] [2410.03594] [manual-01c535c108].
- Several direct email studies depend heavily on Enron or Avocado, so period, organizational culture, sampling procedures, and corpus-specific language may limit generalization [doi-10-1145-3289600-3290984] [doi-10-1145-3331184-3331260] [manual-d19b0f6f1e].
- Several meeting studies use small datasets, simulations, human transcripts, or manually supplied dialogue-act features [doi-10-1007-11965152-18] [doi-10-3115-1564535-1564540] [doi-10-3115-1708376-1708410].
- Many papers evaluate detection or classification rather than complete argument-level extraction. Their F1 values consequently measure different targets and should not be interpreted as directly comparable end-to-end structured-extraction scores.
- Owner, deadline, condition, negation, evidence-span, and decision studies validate separate aspects of the problem. Their combination into one structured workplace extraction scheme is untested.
- Some source analyses identify missing capabilities as limitations rather than experimentally measured findings. This synthesis uses those absences only to delimit what the reported task and evaluation establish, not as positive empirical results.
- Topic 04's unresolved proposition-level scope gaps remain external to this synthesis. A correct Topic 05 act label does not establish that an approval, rejection, request, or commitment was attached to the correct antecedent proposition.
