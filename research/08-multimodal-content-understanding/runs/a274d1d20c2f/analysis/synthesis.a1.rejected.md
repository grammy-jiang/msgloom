# Executive Summary

The corpus provides strong evidence that preserving document structure, layout, and explicit evidence localization improves performance on several document, table, spreadsheet, and long-document benchmarks, while distributed evidence, complex tables, workbook-scale reasoning, and upstream localization remain difficult. Fine-grained provenance has been demonstrated separately at page, quote, cell, bounding-box, segmentation-mask, and answer-span levels. Version-aware retrieval, cross-version element alignment, and temporally verified answering have also been demonstrated, but in different technical-documentation, scientific-PDF, and clinical-guideline domains. No admitted work validates the complete attachment-version-to-answer-span provenance hierarchy requested by this topic. Likewise, direct grounding from workplace email expressions into attachment regions remains unevaluated. Spreadsheet work now covers multi-sheet workbooks and end-to-end workflows, but the broader structural cases in the prior gap remain only partially covered. All eight carried gaps therefore remain open.

# Themes

The first recurring theme is that preserving structure matters. TUTA reports benefits from tree-based attention and tree-position embeddings for structured tables [2010.12537]. TAPAS reports substantial degradation when table-specific pretraining is removed and explicitly retains row-column structure during serialization [doi-10-18653-v1-2020-acl-main-398]. DocFormer reports gains from spatial features and multimodal self-attention over less structured ablations [2106.11539]. LongDocURL reports large downstream improvements when a structure-preserving parsed representation replaces plain PyMuPDF text [2412.18424]. These experiments cover different datasets and structures, but all directly test richer structural representation against a weaker alternative.

A second theme is the progressive refinement of evidence provenance. Multi-page systems such as Hi-VT5 explicitly identify the page containing answer evidence [2212.05935]. MMDocRAG annotates page and quote provenance [2505.16470]. TAPAS selects supporting table cells before aggregation [doi-10-18653-v1-2020-acl-main-398]. RefChartQA grounds answers to chart bounding boxes [2503.23131]. LAT associates reasoning steps and final answers with page indices and bounding boxes [2511.12003]. M3Grounder links individual generated answer spans to phrase-, line-, and block-level segmentation masks [manual-0661fc0cba]. These papers validate separate granularities; they do not jointly validate a single hierarchy spanning attachment version through answer span.

A third theme is that multi-page localization remains both useful and fragile. Hi-VT5 improves realistic multi-page answering and provides answer-page predictions, but its compressed page summaries lose information relative to a full oracle answer page [2212.05935]. DocTrace reports large answer improvements from structured parsing and evidence-graph reasoning, while its error analysis identifies Stage-1 page localization as a major remaining bottleneck, particularly for multi-page questions [2608.03292].

Version-aware processing has also moved beyond static-document QA, but the evidence is split across distinct domains. VersionRAG explicitly represents document versions and changes and substantially outperforms its tested version-agnostic baselines on a small technical-documentation benchmark, while implicit changes remain much harder than explicit version queries [2510.08109]. The scientific-document differencing system aligns typed text, table, formula, and figure elements between revisions and shows a large ablation loss when cross-version alignment is removed [2607.14117]. SentryLine retains document/page citation information and reports temporal drift only when clinical-guideline passages explicitly document a change; removing its temporal verifier substantially increases one reported error rate [2609.08364]. These results cover version identity, element lineage, and temporal verification separately.

Workbook-scale spreadsheet understanding is directly represented in the newer corpus. SpreadsheetAgent separates workbook-structure extraction from task reasoning, and its verification and explicit structured representations improve reported benchmark results [2604.12282]. SpreadsheetBench 2 evaluates complete business workflows across workbooks averaging many sheets and hundreds of target modifications; its strongest evaluated model completes only about one third of tasks exactly, while local cell-modification accuracy can be much higher [2606.29955]. The benchmark's analysis identifies insufficient workbook inspection and wrong target-cell selection as major failure categories [2606.29955].

Distributed evidence is consistently harder than localized evidence. MMLongBench-Doc reports poorer performance on cross-page than single-page questions and deterioration when evidence occurs later in a document [2407.01523]. LongDocURL includes substantial multi-page and cross-element evidence and reports that current systems remain far below its human baseline [2412.18424]. MMDocRAG reports weaker performance on interpretative, multi-page, and multi-image questions even when candidate evidence is supplied [2505.16470].

Several studies also show that flattening a document can discard useful information. MMLongBench-Doc finds OCR-based pipelines competitive for text and table questions but weak on image- and chart-dependent questions [2407.01523]. LongDocURL reports large improvements from preserving table structure in parsed text [2412.18424]. MMDocRAG reports that detailed visual descriptions outperform OCR-only representations of visual evidence in its aggregate comparison [2505.16470].

Upstream evidence failures repeatedly propagate downstream. Table-structure recovery depends on initial cell detection [2111.07129]. MMDocRAG shows substantial answer degradation when perfect evidence candidates are replaced with actual retrieval output [2505.16470]. VersionRAG attributes failures to query misclassification and incomplete change extraction [2510.08109]. Language-based document attribution remains bounded by parser coverage and quote-to-region assignment [2607.24651]. DocTrace similarly reports strong dependence on successful page localization [2608.03292].

Groundedness also changes how systems are evaluated. SMuDGE reports leaderboard changes when spatial groundedness and answer-type awareness are added to scoring, a stronger association between score stability and ranking stability than ANLS, and majority human preference for its ordering in evaluated disagreement cases [doi-10-18653-v1-2025-findings-naacl-295]. Its experiments are limited to extractive single-span answers, so they do not establish equivalent behavior for arbitrary multi-span or abstractive provenance.

Finally, adjacent-domain grounding provides partial transfer evidence for multimodal references. Joint textual and multimodal reference modeling improves pronoun grounding in Japanese visually grounded dialogue [doi-10-18653-v1-2025-acl-long-547]. Scene-text-oriented referring-expression work explicitly connects referring language, recognized text, and visual regions in natural images [doi-10-1109-tmm-2022-3219642] [manual-1048d6caf2]. Neither setting directly tests email text referring into a separate workplace attachment.

# Contradictions

**Visual features do not have a uniform effect.** DocFormer reports improved FUNSD and CORD extraction when image information is added to text and spatial features [2106.11539]. InfographicVQA instead reports that adding visual features to OCR tokens did not improve its LayoutLM baseline, and M4C configurations without detected-object regions or with one full-image feature outperformed configurations using bottom-up object regions [2104.12756]. The architectures and document domains differ, so these results support model- and representation-specific effects rather than a universal claim that adding visual features helps.

**Direct geometric evidence output has conflicting evidence.** RefChartQA demonstrates trainable bounding-box attribution for charts [2503.23131], LAT reports large gains in IoU-based attribution after explicit evidence training [2511.12003], and M3Grounder reports strong mask-grounding results [manual-0661fc0cba]. In contrast, the coordinate-free attribution study finds that direct coordinate output from six evaluated open VLMs has very low evidence recall and very high attribution hallucination, while textual evidence quotations followed by external localization perform substantially better [2607.24651]. The experiments differ in supervision, benchmark, and representation, so coordinate generation cannot be treated as uniformly effective or ineffective.

**Grounding supervision does not uniformly improve answer accuracy.** LAT reports simultaneous gains in answering and grounding for its trained Qwen2.5-VL-based system [2511.12003]. RefChartQA reports large answer gains for TinyChart and some Qwen configurations, but it also reports answer-accuracy losses for UniChart on all three evaluated splits, substantial losses for ChartGemma on some splits, and declines for Qwen2.5-VL on two splits [2503.23131]. The corpus therefore contradicts a universal claim that adding explicit grounding supervision improves answering.

**Direct multimodal input versus OCR/text representations remains context dependent.** MMLongBench-Doc reports that most evaluated LVLMs perform worse overall than LLM counterparts supplied with OCR-derived text, with GPT-4o and GPT-4V as exceptions [2407.01523]. MMDocRAG reports that detailed visual descriptions outperform OCR-only visual representations and that visual and hybrid retrieval are stronger for image-dependent evidence [2505.16470]. These experiments are not directly commensurate, but their results point in different directions on the aggregate value of direct visual processing and should not be silently averaged into one modality preference.

# Open Gaps

**gap-1 — ACADEMIC, HIGH — open.** The corpus now contains version-aware retrieval, cross-version heterogeneous-element alignment, and temporally verified answering, but it still does not validate a single method for maintaining workplace attachment provenance across successive revisions with stable attachment identity, version-relative source locations, semantic change representation, and lineage from an older cited region to its later counterpart. VersionRAG covers explicit versions and change retrieval in technical documentation [2510.08109]; the scientific-document method covers typed element alignment but explicitly has difficulty with splits, merges, and major restructuring [2607.14117]; SentryLine covers temporally verified document/page evidence in clinical guidelines [2609.08364]. Suggested query: `"document version comparison" multimodal provenance semantic change region grounding attachment revision lineage split merge`.

**gap-2 — ACADEMIC, HIGH — open.** Direct grounding of workplace-message expressions such as “this screenshot”, “the highlighted row”, or “numbers below” from email or chat text into a separate attachment, cell, page element, or visual region remains unevaluated. The admitted transfer evidence comes from visually grounded dialogue [doi-10-18653-v1-2025-acl-long-547] and natural-scene scene-text referring expressions [doi-10-1109-tmm-2022-3219642] [manual-1048d6caf2]. Suggested query: `"multimodal coreference" document attachment visual grounding referring expression email table screenshot cross-document`.

**gap-3 — ACADEMIC, HIGH — open.** The corpus now directly covers workbook-level spreadsheet structure and multi-sheet business workflows [2604.12282] [2606.29955], so this gap has narrowed substantially. It remains open because the admitted analyses do not establish comprehensive handling of formulas, hidden rows and sheets, cross-sheet references, merged regions, formatting semantics, units, named ranges, and grounded lineage through those structures. Suggested query: `"spreadsheet understanding" formulas hidden sheets named ranges merged cells cross-sheet references formatting semantics units provenance`.

**gap-4 — ACADEMIC, HIGH — open.** No admitted paper validates an end-to-end provenance representation jointly identifying attachment version, page, element, exact region or cell, and answer span. VersionRAG covers version identity [2510.08109]; scientific-PDF differencing covers cross-version element alignment [2607.14117]; Hi-VT5 covers page provenance [2212.05935]; MMDocRAG covers page and quote evidence [2505.16470]; TAPAS covers table cells [doi-10-18653-v1-2020-acl-main-398]; LAT covers page-plus-bounding-box evidence for reasoning and answers [2511.12003]; and M3Grounder links answer spans to masks [manual-0661fc0cba]. Their combination remains untested. Suggested query: `"document evidence grounding" provenance version page element region cell answer span multimodal citation localization`.

**gap-5 — ENGINEERING, HIGH — open.** End-to-end behavior when extraction or grounding is unsupported, incomplete, or wrong remains insufficiently exercised. MMLongBench-Doc contains deliberately unanswerable questions [2407.01523], LAT evaluates no-answer behavior [2511.12003], and the coordinate-free attribution and DocTrace analyses expose parser, attribution, localization, and irrelevant-evidence failures [2607.24651] [2608.03292]. None supplies a complete operational distinction among true absence, unsupported content, OCR failure, parser failure, layout failure, retrieval failure, and grounding failure.

**gap-6 — ENGINEERING, HIGH — open.** Relevant robustness evidence exists for business tables with empty cells and strict region overlap [2111.07129], heterogeneous reading order [doi-10-1007-s00521-022-06948-5], long multimodal documents [2412.18424], and complex business workbooks [2606.29955]. The corpus does not provide one heterogeneous workplace-style attachment stress benchmark covering sparse tables, merged structures, OCR corruption, irregular reading order, embedded images, scans, and mixed document formats.

**gap-7 — ENGINEERING, MEDIUM — open.** Scaling limitations remain visible rather than resolved. Pairwise table-structure inference becomes expensive as cell counts grow [2111.07129]. Hi-VT5 encounters page-count-related training memory pressure and loses information through page compression [2212.05935]. LongDocURL uses cut-off or merge strategies for models unable to consume complete long documents [2412.18424]. MMDocRAG observes evidence-position bias [2505.16470]. SpreadsheetAgent averages many agent calls and substantial extraction latency per workbook [2604.12282].

**gap-8 — ENGINEERING, HIGH — open.** Groundedness evaluation [doi-10-18653-v1-2025-findings-naacl-295], multimodal long-document QA [2407.01523] [2412.18424], spreadsheet workflow evaluation [2606.29955], and reading-order benchmarks [doi-10-1007-s00521-022-06948-5] each cover relevant components. No admitted evaluation simultaneously scores semantic correctness, extraction completeness, exact provenance, unsupported-evidence handling, and preservation of tables, images, and reading order on workplace-style attachments.

# Actionable Insights

- **Synthesis-derived recommendation:** represent provenance as composable layers rather than one opaque citation identifier, so attachment/version, page or sheet, element, region or cell, and answer span can be recorded independently and combined when available.
- **Synthesis-derived recommendation:** keep cross-version correspondence as an explicit lineage relation with confidence and change type rather than overwriting an older region identifier with its presumed successor.
- **Synthesis-derived recommendation:** preserve both semantic structure and source geometry during extraction; table and spreadsheet representations should retain row, column, hierarchy, sheet, and region information separately from textual values.
- **Synthesis-derived recommendation:** treat email-to-attachment reference grounding as a separate operation from subsequent proposition or action-scope interpretation, because the current corpus does not directly validate collapsing those operations.
- **Synthesis-derived recommendation:** expose distinct states for no evidence found, evidence unsupported by the extractor, extraction failure, localization failure, and conflicting evidence rather than mapping all of them to an empty result.
- **Synthesis-derived recommendation:** evaluate provenance at several granularities independently—version, page or sheet, element, region or cell, and answer span—so a correct coarse citation cannot conceal an incorrect fine-grained citation.
- **Synthesis-derived recommendation:** include adversarial revision cases such as pagination shifts, moved tables, split or merged elements, renamed files, reordered sheets, and locally unchanged evidence inside globally changed documents when engineering cross-version lineage tests.
- **Synthesis-derived recommendation:** for spreadsheet evaluation, score complete-workbook semantics in addition to target-cell accuracy, including whether the correct sheet, range, formula dependencies, units, and structural context were used.
- **Synthesis-derived recommendation:** benchmark at least two evidence-attribution interfaces rather than assuming direct coordinate generation is optimal; quotation-plus-localization and direct region prediction have shown materially different failure profiles.
- **Synthesis-derived recommendation:** maintain stage-level provenance and diagnostics through parsing, retrieval, localization, reasoning, and answer generation so downstream citation failures can be attributed to the stage that introduced them.

# Confidence Summary

Confidence is **HIGH** for the recurring evidence that document/table structure, explicit evidence localization, and upstream evidence quality materially affect benchmark performance, because these conclusions recur across multiple full-text studies and controlled ablations [2010.12537] [2106.11539] [2412.18424] [2505.16470] [2608.03292]. Confidence is also **HIGH** that version-aware retrieval [2510.08109], cross-version element alignment [2607.14117], workbook-level reasoning [2604.12282] [2606.29955], and fine-grained grounding [2503.23131] [2511.12003] [manual-0661fc0cba] have each been demonstrated in their respective domains.

Confidence is **MEDIUM** for transfer from visually grounded dialogue or natural-scene referring expressions to workplace email attachments because those experiments are adjacent-domain rather than direct [doi-10-18653-v1-2025-acl-long-547] [doi-10-1109-tmm-2022-3219642]. The corpus therefore supports several well-supported design principles, but it does not empirically validate the complete combined version-aware workplace provenance scheme.

# Limitations

- The corpus spans scientific PDFs, technical documentation, clinical guidelines, web tables, charts, infographics, natural scenes, business spreadsheets, and general long-document QA. These provide transfer evidence but are not direct evidence of production workplace email behavior.
- Several relevant analyses are abstract-only or rely partly on official project pages, especially [manual-0661fc0cba], [manual-1048d6caf2], [manual-7fdf40b3c6], and [doi-10-1109-tmm-2022-3219642], limiting independent inspection of detailed ablations and author-stated limitations.
- No admitted study evaluates naturally occurring email referring expressions against successively revised attachments, so the email-to-attachment portion of the target problem remains unsupported by direct evidence.
- The cross-version papers use different notions of versioning: technical-documentation graph versions [2510.08109], scientific-PDF element alignment [2607.14117], and explicitly documented clinical-guideline changes [2609.08364]. Their results cannot be treated as validation of one integrated lineage model.
- Provenance studies use incompatible granularities and representations, including pages, quotes, cells, bounding boxes, segmentation masks, and evidence graphs, limiting direct cross-paper metric comparison.
- Some long-document evaluations use model-based answer extraction, model judges, or model-generated supervision [2407.01523] [2412.18424] [2505.16470] [2608.03292], introducing additional evaluator or teacher-model dependencies.
- The inherited Topic 04 and Topic 05 handoffs were used only to scope this review. They are project context, not evidence for any finding or gap-status decision in this synthesis.
- The carried gaps were assessed only against the 28 admitted analyses supplied here. No external papers, product documentation, or implementation benchmarks were used to close them.
