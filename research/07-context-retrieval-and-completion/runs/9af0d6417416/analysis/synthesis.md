# Executive Summary

Across 17 distinct papers, the strongest recurring evidence is that retrieval benefits from being conditioned on the current information need rather than run once or on a fixed schedule. Conversational rewriting, forward-looking queries, reasoning-conditioned retrieval, and structured gap queries each improve retrieval or QA in their tested domains. Several studies also show that excessive retrieval can add noise, so adaptive triggering and stopping matter. Newer work makes evidence sufficiency explicit: sufficiency judgments can improve abstention, guide another retrieval step, and reduce searches, but calibration errors and unsafe early stops remain material. Permission-aware RAG demonstrates per-document IAM enforcement before generation, yet does not test gap-driven replenishment after filtered evidence. Temporal-search work shows that time intent can affect relevance, but version lineage and temporal validity are not jointly handled. No study validates the full combined workplace loop.

# Themes

The corpus contains 17 distinct supplied papers.

**Context-sensitive and feedback-conditioned query formulation.** Conversational rewriting improves retrieval over the evaluated supervised rewriting baseline and is especially useful after topic shifts [2112.08558]. FLARE reports better results when queries are formed from anticipated future content rather than already generated previous text [2305.06983]. DRAGIN separately evaluates retrieval timing and query formulation and reports gains from information-need-aware timing and self-attention-based query selection [2403.10081]. ToolQP likewise improves tool retrieval by decomposing requests and conditioning later queries on earlier retrieval feedback [doi-10-18653-v1-2026-findings-acl-2090]. These studies use different tasks and mechanisms, so they do not establish one universally best query strategy.

**Iterative retrieval for multi-step evidence acquisition.** Self-ask demonstrates an iterative pattern in which a model generates a follow-up question, receives an external answer, and can repeat the process before answering [2210.03350]. IRCoT reports large fixed-budget retrieval-recall gains from alternating reasoning and retrieval across several multi-hop QA datasets, with downstream QA gains in most but not every setting [2212.10509]. S2G-RAG makes the iteration explicitly gap-driven and reports stronger QA results than its compared systems on TriviaQA, HotpotQA, and 2WikiMultiHopQA [2604.23783].

**Adaptive retrieval timing versus fixed or always-retrieve policies.** FLARE reports that increasing retrieval frequency does not monotonically improve results and can degrade StrategyQA performance [2305.06983]. DRAGIN finds that fixed-interval and fixed-sentence dynamic retrieval do not consistently outperform single-round RAG [2403.10081]. SeaKR's ablation likewise shows lower performance when retrieval occurs at every generation step rather than being triggered adaptively [2406.19215]. These are mutually consistent results from different QA or generation settings.

**Evidence sufficiency, abstention, and stopping.** A context-sufficiency classifier can distinguish retrieval insufficiency from downstream model failure, and combining sufficiency with answer confidence improves selective generation in the reported experiments [2411.06037]. S2G-RAG uses an explicit sufficiency judgment to decide whether to retrieve again and reports relatively few false declarations of sufficiency under its supporting-title proxy, although it also continues retrieving for many proxy-sufficient cases [2604.23783]. AB-RAG reports that confidence can separate correct from incorrect answers and drive a bounded retrieve-or-stop loop [2606.29090]. A dedicated stopping study, however, shows that a structured judge can reduce searches while still executing a substantial fraction of unsafe early stops [2608.13237].

**Structured unresolved evidence gaps.** S2G-RAG directly tests a controller in which insufficiency emits structured gap items such as target, slot, and description, which are converted into new retrieval queries. Removing the judge produces the largest reported component-ablation loss on HotpotQA with BM25. The same paper states that its representation is weak for temporal constraints and some complex compositional relations [2604.23783].

**Retrieval budgeting and total system cost are different questions.** Calibrated budget allocation distinguishes retrieval calls, passage budget, and full-context usage, and shows that retrieving one passage can save context while still incurring the same retrieval-call cost [2606.29959]. The same study finds that a gating probe can make an adaptive system slower than always-RAG for one tested model while producing a latency benefit for another [2606.29959]. The Search-R1 stopping study similarly cautions that reducing searches does not establish lower total cost because the stopping judge adds computation, memory, and latency [2608.13237]. CORAL also identifies agentic planning and criticism as an inference-cost source [2604.25676].

**Permission enforcement before generation.** Permission-Aware RAG validates each semantically retrieved candidate against its provider's native IAM system before authorized evidence is passed to the LLM, and reports exact permission-match success across eight experimental permission profiles [doi-10-1109-access-2025-3628960]. Its evaluation nevertheless uses fixed top-k retrieval followed by authorization filtering; it does not test another evidence-gap-driven retrieval when required support is removed by permissions [doi-10-1109-access-2025-3628960].

**Temporal intent affects relevance.** Temporal-profile features improve temporal-query classification relative to majority baselines on several evaluated news and search-log collections, although effectiveness varies materially by collection [doi-10-1145-1247715-1247720]. Query-log-derived temporal reordering improves DCG@5 only for the strongest implicitly temporal query group in its reported commercial-search evaluation, with negligible or negative changes for lower-confidence groups [doi-10-1145-1571941-1572085]. These studies address temporal intent, not document-version validity.

**Retrieved-context sufficiency and answer correctness are distinct.** Models can fail even when retrieved context is judged sufficient and can sometimes answer correctly with context judged insufficient [2411.06037]. Permission-Aware RAG similarly reports non-zero QA performance for a profile with zero document permission coverage, showing that the absence of authorized retrieved evidence does not itself cause a generator to abstain [doi-10-1109-access-2025-3628960].

**Adaptive retrieval depends on reader and retriever capability.** AB-RAG reports adaptive Exact Match gains for some tested model/dataset combinations but no gain for its smallest Qwen model [2606.29090]. Calibrated retrieval-budget allocation finds that larger readers can skip more retrieval on TriviaQA while maintaining always-RAG accuracy, while low closed-book headroom drives policies toward near-always retrieval on Natural Questions and MS MARCO [2606.29959]. The same study reports that sufficiently poor retriever quality bounds achievable answer accuracy regardless of the gating policy [2606.29959].

# Contradictions

No direct contradiction met the required standard of two papers answering the same question under comparable conditions.

Several apparent tensions are scope qualifications rather than contradictions. CONQRR finds that raw dialogue context can work well when a conversation remains tightly on-topic but degrades after topic shifts, while learned rewriting is more robust to those shifts [2112.08558]; these are two conditions within the same analysis, not conflicting studies. SeaKR reports harm from always retrieving [2406.19215], while calibrated budget allocation selects near-always retrieval for some datasets with very weak closed-book performance [2606.29959]; the tasks, policies, and evidence regimes differ, so these results do not answer the same experimental question. IRCoT also reports a large IIRC retrieval-recall improvement without a corresponding QA gain [2212.10509], which qualifies rather than contradicts the broader evidence that better retrieval often improves downstream QA.

# Open Gaps

**G1 — ACADEMIC, HIGH.** Structured gap representations remain insufficiently validated for temporal constraints, multi-entity joins, and complex compositional evidence requirements. S2G-RAG explicitly identifies temporal constraints and more complex relations as limitations of its schema [2604.23783].  
Suggested query: `"structured evidence gaps" temporal constraints multi-hop retrieval OR "iterative retrieval" compositional gap representation`

**G2 — ACADEMIC, HIGH.** No admitted study validates an iterative evidence-gap retrieval loop that jointly preserves document-version lineage, freshness or effective-time validity, and structured sufficiency decisions. The iterative RAG papers generally omit version lineage [2604.23783] [2604.25676] [2606.29959], while the admitted temporal-retrieval papers model temporal query relevance rather than document-version validity [doi-10-1145-1247715-1247720] [doi-10-1145-1571941-1572085].  
Suggested query: `"version-aware retrieval" provenance temporal RAG iterative retrieval OR "temporal validity" document versions retrieval augmented generation`

**G3 — ACADEMIC, HIGH.** Stopping reliability under domain shift remains open. Sufficiency and confidence signals can improve selective decisions [2411.06037] [2606.29090], but conservative over-retrieval remains in S2G-RAG [2604.23783], calibration can drift after domain shift [2606.29959], and the dedicated Search-R1 study reports unsafe executed early stops and poor transfer of validation stopping precision [2608.13237]. No admitted study provides a general bounded-risk stopping guarantee.  
Suggested query: `"RAG stopping" calibrated risk selective prediction domain shift OR "safe early stopping" retrieval augmented generation`

**G4 — ENGINEERING, HIGH.** The corpus does not test a bounded replenishment loop in which unauthorized candidates are filtered through native IAM, evidence sufficiency is reassessed, and another permission-constrained query is issued when authorized evidence remains incomplete. Permission-aware filtering is demonstrated with fixed top-k retrieval [doi-10-1109-access-2025-3628960], while iterative gap-directed replenishment is demonstrated without IAM constraints [2604.23783].

**G5 — ENGINEERING, HIGH.** The evaluated systems do not jointly distinguish empty retrieval, authorization denial, unavailable source, unsupported extraction, exhausted budget, and true negative evidence as separate terminal or retry conditions. The permission paper exercises authorization outcomes [doi-10-1109-access-2025-3628960], and iterative systems exercise sufficiency and budget stopping [2604.23783] [2608.13237], but the combined failure-state semantics are not evaluated.

**G6 — ENGINEERING, MEDIUM.** An end-to-end cost evaluation covering retrieval calls, retrieved passages or tokens, planner or judge inference, reranking, permission-check latency, and total wall-clock latency is still absent. Individual papers quantify parts of this problem, including adaptive inference cost [2604.25676], confidence-driven retrieval iterations [2606.29090], distinct retrieval budget axes and latency [2606.29959], stopping-judge overhead [2608.13237], and IAM latency [doi-10-1109-access-2025-3628960].

**G7 — ENGINEERING, HIGH.** The corpus lacks a workplace-communication evaluation combining explicit evidence gaps with heterogeneous messages and documents, multi-hop dependencies, permission restrictions, stale or conflicting evidence, and bounded retrieval. Conversational retrieval is evaluated on CAsT and OR-QuAC [2105.04166], iterative reasoning on multi-hop QA [2212.10509] [2604.23783], and permission enforcement on a HotpotQA-derived multi-resource prototype [doi-10-1109-access-2025-3628960]; none directly evaluates the combined workplace setting.

**G8 — ACADEMIC, HIGH.** No admitted study validates a single controller that jointly handles structured evidence gaps, permission-aware source access, version lineage, temporal validity, calibrated stopping, abstention, and total-cost budgeting. Structured gap retrieval [2604.23783], budgeted selective retrieval [2606.29959], and native-IAM filtering [doi-10-1109-access-2025-3628960] each cover different parts of the problem; their combination is untested.  
Suggested query: `"enterprise RAG" iterative retrieval permissions provenance temporal version stopping OR "permission-aware RAG" adaptive retrieval sufficiency`

# Actionable Insights

- **Synthesis-derived recommendation:** represent an investigation request as an explicit evidence gap with retrieval scope and budget metadata rather than only as a rewritten free-text query.
- **Synthesis-derived recommendation:** make the decisions “is more evidence needed?”, “what should be fetched next?”, and “is the accumulated evidence sufficient?” separately observable and separately testable.
- **Synthesis-derived recommendation:** apply authorization before evidence reaches generation, then reassess sufficiency and perform only bounded additional retrieval when permitted evidence remains incomplete.
- **Synthesis-derived recommendation:** preserve distinct outcomes for no match, denied access, unavailable source, extraction failure, budget exhaustion, and evidence that genuinely supports a negative conclusion.
- **Synthesis-derived recommendation:** retain a hard retrieval cap in addition to any learned sufficiency or confidence signal, and do not treat a generated final-answer marker as equivalent to evidence completeness.
- **Synthesis-derived recommendation:** keep the original question or evidence gap available as an anchor across iterative query reformulation so retrieval feedback cannot silently redefine the task.
- **Synthesis-derived recommendation:** measure retrieval calls, passages or tokens, model and judge inference, IAM checks, and end-to-end latency separately before optimizing any single cost metric.
- **Synthesis-derived recommendation:** build adversarial evaluation fixtures for denied reads, empty searches, stale and conflicting versions, partial access, multi-hop dependencies, budget exhaustion, and unsafe early stopping.

# Confidence Summary

The corpus provides well-supported design principles for context-sensitive query formulation, iterative retrieval, adaptive retrieval timing, explicit sufficiency assessment, bounded stopping, and permission filtering within the domains actually tested. Confidence is high that these mechanisms can improve retrieval or QA metrics in conversational search, Wikipedia-based QA, and related retrieval tasks [2112.08558] [2212.10509] [2305.06983] [2403.10081] [2604.23783]. Confidence is medium for transfer to workplace communication because most evidence is indirect and heterogeneous. Confidence is low for any claim that the mechanisms already form a reliable integrated enterprise investigation loop: permissions, document versions, temporal validity, provenance, calibrated stopping, and total cost have not been jointly validated [2606.29959] [2608.13237] [doi-10-1109-access-2025-3628960].

# Limitations

- Most evidence comes from QA, conversational search, Wikipedia retrieval, or tool retrieval rather than production workplace email and messaging.
- The permission-aware study is closer to enterprise access control than the other papers, but its quantitative corpus is HotpotQA-derived and its fixed-top-k evaluation does not establish workplace retrieval behavior [doi-10-1109-access-2025-3628960].
- The temporal studies address temporal query intent and timestamp distributions rather than document revision lineage, effective-time semantics, or historical validity of workplace evidence [doi-10-1145-1247715-1247720] [doi-10-1145-1571941-1572085].
- Several adaptive-retrieval methods rely on model internals such as token probabilities, self-attention, or hidden states, so their experimental results do not automatically transfer to closed APIs [2305.06983] [2403.10081] [2406.19215].
- Some recent studies use restricted datasets, synthetic trajectories, small calibration sets, or system-specific probes, limiting external validity [2411.06037] [2606.29090] [2608.13237] [doi-10-18653-v1-2026-findings-acl-2090].
- Papers validating different components do not jointly validate a combined permission-, version-, temporal-, cost-, and sufficiency-aware retrieval system.
- No duplicate studies were detected among the 17 supplied titles, so the paper count reflects 17 distinct supplied works.
- Inherited handoffs in the project context were treated only as scoping information and were not used as evidence for findings or gaps.
- No prior-round gap list or prior gap identifiers were supplied with this synthesis input; therefore `gap_status` is empty rather than inventing prior gap state.
