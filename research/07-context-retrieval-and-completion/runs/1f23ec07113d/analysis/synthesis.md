# Executive Summary

Across 45 supplied paper records, the literature supports component-level evidence for adaptive retrieval, structured sufficiency and gap judging, temporal and version-aware retrieval, permission-gated access, and calibrated risk control. Adaptive and interleaved RAG often improve multi-hop evidence recall or answer quality, yet retrieval utility is heterogeneous and can add noise or harm some queries. Structured stopping can reduce retrieval, but calibration can fail to transfer and unsafe early stops remain. Temporal and version-aware methods materially improve retrieval on evolving corpora, while permission-aware systems demonstrate access filtering in synthetic or constructed enterprise settings. Conformal methods provide risk guarantees under stated assumptions and specialized adaptations improve coverage under shift. No admitted paper validates the full joint controller described by this topic, and none closes the carried-over gaps involving combined provenance, permissions, temporal validity, distribution-shift-aware stopping, workplace communication, or complete cost accounting.

# Themes

**1. Adaptive retrieval is useful but not monotonically beneficial.** Across multi-hop and knowledge-intensive QA experiments, adaptive or interleaved retrieval frequently improves retrieval recall and/or answer quality relative to one-shot, fixed-interval, or indiscriminate retrieval [2212.10509] [2305.06983] [2403.10081] [2406.19215]. The effect varies by task and model, and retrieval can harm individual queries even when always-retrieve improves aggregate accuracy [2607.24010]. Confidence: **HIGH**.

**2. Structured sufficiency and evidence gaps provide an explicit retrieval-control signal, but stopping remains imperfect.** S2G-RAG represents unresolved information as typed gap items and converts them into later retrieval queries, with strong reported multi-hop QA results [2604.23783]. A structured stopping judge applied to frozen Search-R1 trajectories reduced mean searches by 3.70% while losing 0.625 percentage points of exact match within its prespecified noninferiority margin, but 39.13% of executed early stops were unsafe under the paper's probe definition [2608.13237]. Confidence: **HIGH**.

**3. Retrieval success and answerability are different variables.** Naturally occurring QA can appear unanswerable because evidence retrieval or source coverage failed, and some document-relative unanswerable cases become answerable when a broader source is searched [2010.11915]. Context-sufficiency assessment separates insufficient retrieval from generation failure despite adequate evidence [2411.06037], and set-level sufficiency verification improves selective ranking in a controlled RAG benchmark [2605.03534]. Confidence: **HIGH**.

**4. Explicit temporal and version modeling materially improves retrieval over evolving corpora.** MRAG improves time-sensitive evidence retrieval and downstream QA relative to standard retrieval pipelines [2412.15540]. VersionRAG reports large gains over naive RAG and GraphRAG on version-sensitive questions [2510.08109]. Temporal GraphRAG attributes a large ablation loss to removal of temporal retrieval [2510.13590], while TimelyRAG improves sparse and dense retrievers on overlapping evolving documents using semantic-temporal reranking [2609.11572]. Confidence: **HIGH**.

**5. Permission-aware retrieval is technically feasible in evaluated enterprise-like settings, but those evaluations do not cover the full investigation loop.** Secure RAG work protects private text and embeddings and enforces authenticated isolation [2508.01084]. PPPARITL performs authorization filtering before similarity-based use of candidates in a synthetic enterprise setting [doi-10-1038-s41598-026-71936-x]. Permission-Aware RAG delegates per-document decisions to native IAM providers before evidence reaches the LLM context [doi-10-1109-access-2025-3628960]. HONEYBEE shows access-aware vector indexing can trade memory against latency and recall [doi-10-1145-3786625]. Confidence: **MEDIUM**, because these are not direct workplace-communication investigation evaluations.

**6. Formal risk control is available under explicit assumptions, and distribution shift can invalidate static calibration.** Weighted conformal prediction restores marginal coverage under covariate shift when the relevant weighting assumptions hold [1904.06019]. Conformal Risk Control extends calibration to bounded monotone expected losses [2208.02814], and anytime-valid risk control permits adaptive stopping under its sequential assumptions [2406.10490]. PromptShift-CRC reports near-target coverage under several prompt/domain shifts where static calibration collapses [2606.15964], while structured-generation experiments likewise show substantially more violations from static calibration under temporal or severity shift than from adaptive conformal procedures [2606.29054]. Confidence: **HIGH**.

**7. Stopping controllers reduce retrieval or reasoning expenditure under benchmark-specific trade-offs, but they use different objectives and guarantees.** Stop-RAG uses learned trajectory value [2510.14337]; MiCP calibrates retrieval, stopping, and answer sets with conformal machinery [2604.01413]; TASR combines answer stability with calibrated confidence without training a controller [2606.13814]; and RAG-on-a-Diet jointly selects model resources and termination under a cost-sensitive RL objective [doi-10-18653-v1-2026-acl-long-1562]. These papers report reduced turns, calls, or cost with maintained benchmark-specific constraints or explicit quality reductions. Confidence: **MEDIUM**.

**8. Retrieval-budget decisions depend materially on reader capability, dataset, and threshold transfer.** Know Before You Fetch reports that stronger readers can skip much more retrieval on some datasets while datasets with low closed-book headroom still retrieve for nearly all questions [2606.29959]. Budget-aware Active RAG evaluation finds that nominal usage targets can be exceeded on held-out calibration/test splits and that router rankings vary across datasets, budgets, and model families [2607.24010]. Confidence: **HIGH**.

**9. Conflicting evidence is a distinct and unresolved selective-generation failure mode.** CLEAR reports benefits from keeping parametric, curated-local, and dynamically retrieved evidence separate before adjudication in medical QA, although follow-up retrieval changes relatively few answers [2609.16301]. ECR exposes ambiguity rather than an overconfident resolution under its controlled contradiction injection [2603.28444]. In a seven-model sufficiency benchmark, every evaluated model over-answers more than 65% of explicit-conflict cases and evidence picking is the dominant failure pattern [doi-10-32604-cmc-2026-086343]. Confidence: **MEDIUM** because the strongest contradiction-handling results come from medical or controlled benchmark settings.

**10. Query formulation is part of retrieval control rather than a neutral preprocessing step.** ConvDR improves first-stage conversational retrieval over evaluated sparse and automatic-rewrite baselines and retains useful conversational context [2105.04166]. CONQRR improves retrieval metrics by optimizing rewriting against retrieval reward, while its analysis also finds omissions and introduced information in generated rewrites; direct full-dialogue submission degrades sharply after topic shifts [2112.08558]. Confidence: **HIGH**.

# Contradictions

**Stop-RAG consistency claim — [2510.14337].** The single question is whether Stop-RAG consistently outperforms both compared stopping baselines across benchmark settings. The paper's abstract-level statement says it does, but the reported CoRAG HotpotQA result places Stop-RAG slightly below unmodified CoRAG and does not show dominance over LLM-Stop. The narrower benchmark-specific results remain usable; the universal claim does not.

**MiCP prediction-set-size claim — [2604.01413].** The single question is whether MiCP consistently improves prediction-set size relative to the no-early-stopping baseline. The paper's general statement conflicts with several Table 1 rows, including GPT-4o-mini HotpotQA settings in which the proposed method produces larger prediction sets.

Other apparent tensions were not classified as contradictions when the papers answer different questions or use materially different datasets, models, controllers, or metrics. For example, strong aggregate gains from iterative retrieval [2601.19827] and query-level retrieval harm [2607.24010] can both hold simultaneously.

# Open Gaps

**gap-1 — ACADEMIC, HIGH — OPEN.** Structured gap representations remain insufficiently validated for temporal constraints, multi-entity joins, and complex compositional evidence requirements. S2G-RAG provides an explicit target/slot/description gap representation [2604.23783], while temporal retrieval and iterative query planning are treated separately [2412.15540] [doi-10-18653-v1-2026-findings-acl-2090]. No admitted study evaluates those capabilities together.  
Suggested query: `"structured evidence gap" temporal constraints multi-entity joins compositional retrieval iterative RAG`

**gap-2 — ACADEMIC, HIGH — OPEN.** No admitted study validates an iterative evidence-gap retrieval loop that jointly preserves document-version lineage, freshness or effective-time validity, and structured sufficiency decisions. S2G-RAG supplies iterative structured-gap control [2604.23783]; VersionRAG supplies explicit evolving-document version modeling [2510.08109]; Temporal GraphRAG and TimelyRAG supply temporal retrieval mechanisms [2510.13590] [2609.11572]. Their combination is untested.  
Suggested query: `"version-aware retrieval" temporal provenance iterative RAG document lineage freshness effective-time sufficiency`

**gap-3 — ACADEMIC, HIGH — OPEN.** Stopping reliability under domain shift remains unresolved. MiCP calibrates adaptive stopping under its stated assumptions [2604.01413], while PromptShift-CRC and adaptive conformal methods address shifted calibration [2606.15964] [2606.29054]. TASR shows encouraging transfer for a fixed rule [2606.13814], whereas the structured Search-R1 stopper exhibits calibration transfer weakness and unsafe early stops [2608.13237]. No admitted paper supplies a general bounded-risk iterative-RAG stopping guarantee under distribution shift.  
Suggested query: `"iterative RAG" calibrated stopping domain shift conformal risk control selective prediction adaptive retrieval`

**gap-4 — ENGINEERING, HIGH — OPEN.** The corpus does not test a bounded replenishment loop in which unauthorized candidates are filtered through native IAM, evidence sufficiency is reassessed, and another permission-constrained query is issued when authorized evidence remains incomplete. Native-IAM and permission-aware filtering are demonstrated separately [doi-10-1109-access-2025-3628960] [doi-10-1038-s41598-026-71936-x], while S2G-RAG performs iterative sufficiency reassessment without this access-control loop [2604.23783].

**gap-5 — ENGINEERING, HIGH — OPEN.** No evaluated controller jointly distinguishes empty retrieval, authorization denial, unavailable source, unsupported extraction, exhausted budget, and true negative evidence as separate retry or terminal states. The corpus separately studies unanswerability [2010.11915], context sufficiency [2411.06037], structured iterative retrieval [2604.23783], and authorization [doi-10-1109-access-2025-3628960].

**gap-6 — ENGINEERING, MEDIUM — OPEN.** End-to-end cost accounting remains absent. Individual papers report retrieval calls and passage usage [2606.29959] [2607.24010], model-selection and reasoning cost [doi-10-18653-v1-2026-acl-long-1562], temporal-reranking latency [2609.11572], or access-aware indexing latency and memory [doi-10-1145-3786625]. None reports the complete combination of calls, retrieved tokens, planner/judge inference, reranking, authorization checks, and wall-clock latency.

**gap-7 — ENGINEERING, HIGH — OPEN.** The corpus lacks a workplace-communication evaluation combining explicit evidence gaps with heterogeneous messages and documents, multi-hop dependencies, permission restrictions, stale or conflicting evidence, and bounded retrieval. Structured DevOps retrieval is adjacent-domain evidence [2606.05658], and the permission papers use synthetic or constructed enterprise evaluations [doi-10-1109-access-2025-3628960] [doi-10-1038-s41598-026-71936-x]; neither directly evaluates the stated workplace-communication setting.

**gap-8 — ACADEMIC, HIGH — OPEN.** No admitted study validates a single controller jointly handling structured evidence gaps, permission-aware source access, version lineage, temporal validity, calibrated stopping, abstention, and total-cost budgeting. Structured gap control [2604.23783], version awareness [2510.08109], native-IAM filtering [doi-10-1109-access-2025-3628960], distribution-shift-aware calibration [2606.15964], and calibrated budget allocation [2606.29959] are separate component results.  
Suggested query: `"adaptive RAG controller" permission-aware provenance temporal version calibrated stopping abstention budget`

All eight carried-over gaps therefore remain open; none is resolved or downgraded by the current corpus.

# Actionable Insights

- **Synthesis-derived recommendation:** represent an investigation controller state explicitly, including the unresolved evidence gap, authorized search scope, temporal and version constraints, accumulated evidence, unresolved conflicts, remaining budget, and terminal reason.
- **Synthesis-derived recommendation:** define separate terminal and retry outcomes for sufficient evidence, empty retrieval, permission denial, unavailable source, unsupported extraction, unresolved conflict, budget exhaustion, and calibrated abstention rather than collapsing them into a generic not-found state.
- **Synthesis-derived recommendation:** enforce authorization before retrieved material becomes generation context, and allow the controller to reassess sufficiency and issue another bounded query over the remaining authorized scope.
- **Synthesis-derived recommendation:** keep semantic relevance, effective-time validity, document version identity, and retrieval provenance as separately inspectable signals rather than compressing them into one retrieval score.
- **Synthesis-derived recommendation:** calibrate retrieval and stopping thresholds on future-like held-out data, monitor deployment drift, and use a conservative fallback when calibration transfer is outside the validated regime.
- **Synthesis-derived recommendation:** evaluate the controller with independent gold and adversarial fixtures covering multi-hop dependencies, stale versions, conflicting versions, denied reads, empty search, unsupported extraction, partial evidence, and exhausted budgets.
- **Synthesis-derived recommendation:** record retrieval calls, passage and token volume, planner and judge inference, reranking, permission-check overhead, and total wall-clock latency so quality-risk-cost trade-offs can be measured end to end.
- **Synthesis-derived recommendation:** integrate the component mechanisms behind modular interfaces and retain factorial ablations; component-level paper results do not establish correctness of their combined behavior.

# Confidence Summary

The corpus provides well-supported design principles at the component level. Adaptive retrieval [2212.10509] [2403.10081], explicit sufficiency assessment [2411.06037] [2604.23783], temporal and version-aware retrieval [2510.08109] [2609.11572], permission gating [doi-10-1109-access-2025-3628960], and calibrated risk control [2208.02814] [2606.15964] each have positive evidence in their own evaluated settings.

Confidence is **HIGH** for these component-level observations and for the conclusion that retrieval utility and calibration transfer are heterogeneous [2607.24010] [2606.29054]. Confidence is **MEDIUM** for generalizing individual stopping and permission results beyond their reported QA, medical, synthetic-enterprise, or structured-search settings. Confidence is **LOW** for the behavior of the complete joint controller in workplace communication because no admitted study evaluates the full combination under temporal evolution, access restrictions, provenance requirements, cost limits, and distribution shift.

# Limitations

- The supplied material consists of reduced per-paper research questions and top findings rather than full paper texts, so this synthesis cannot independently inspect experimental details omitted from those analyses.
- Most adaptive-retrieval and stopping evidence comes from QA or benchmark research rather than workplace email and Teams communication; those results are transfer evidence rather than direct production-domain evidence.
- Permission-aware results include synthetic or constructed enterprise evaluations and therefore do not establish production behavior under a real heterogeneous workplace permission graph.
- Temporal, version-aware, permission-aware, structured-gap, calibration, and stopping mechanisms are validated mostly in separate systems; combining their individual results would not constitute validation of an integrated controller.
- The inherited handoffs from Topics 03, 04, 05, 06, and 08 were treated only as project context and were not used as evidence for findings or gap closure.
- No duplicate titles are apparent among the 45 supplied records, but the reduced analyses do not permit a full bibliographic check for differently titled or derivative records of the same underlying study.
- Two explicit claim-versus-result inconsistencies are preserved in the Contradictions section rather than averaged into the broader evidence.
- Apparent cross-paper tensions about retrieval benefit, decomposition, or early stopping were treated as scope qualifications when the underlying datasets, models, controllers, or evaluation definitions differed rather than being labeled as contradictions.
