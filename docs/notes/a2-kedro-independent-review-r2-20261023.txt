**BLOCKING FINDINGS**

All three are documentation-only fixes. None needs new Kedro facts, and none blocks the read-only Q0.1-Q0.4 investigation.

**B1. The batch-wide byte budgets and the capture stage are misplaced (P1#2).**
- Location: `design.md:107-112` (step 5 at `:111`), the diagram at `:73-87`, and `:215`.
- The design places the byte budgets in the per-record parse step and calls them all "per-item and aggregate".
- In the code, `max_derived_bytes` is the only per-item limit (`record_steps.py:383`). `max_total_selected_bytes`, `max_total_derived_bytes` and `max_total_parser_output_bytes` are batch aggregates (`record_steps.py:381-386`, `:457-459`).
- The selection budget is a whole-batch pre-pass in `handler.py:289-352`. It counts the encoded selection (or the saved byte_count in replay mode) plus the unique declared evidence bytes (`handler.py:355-368`). It runs before any parse. In live mode `collected_selection@1` is saved only after the whole pass (`:338-352`).
- Failure is `FAILED` with zero prepared results.
- A separate replay preflight (`handler.py:221-250`) returns `BLOCKED` before the claim is acquired, with `invalid-replay-plan`, `invalid-replay-result` or `selection-budget-exceeded`.
- A per-record running total would publish earlier prepared results and then fail, which changes legacy behaviour.
- The design also lists a parser "CPU" ceiling. The parser limits in `parsed_identity.py:61-67` are wall time, memory, decompressed bytes, output bytes and container members.
- Fix:
  - Add an explicit capture and preflight step to §5.
  - State the whole-batch aggregate rule and the BLOCKED paths.
  - Correct the "per-item" wording and the CPU item.

**B2. The partial-result order and intra-record behaviour are an undeclared policy change (P1#4).**
- Location: `design.md:71`, `:79-81`, `:132`, `:134`.
- The design says it "preserves the legacy partial-result lifecycle". It does not.
- Legacy publishes all prepared results, then all filter results (`handler.py:124-133`). The new design publishes prepared then filter per record, after the two-node DAG returns.
- The oracle test `tests/phase1_foundation/test_preparation_intake_plan_acceptance.py:18-28` forces a filter-stage failure and requires exactly `("prepared",)` to be retained. Under the new design a failure in the filter node publishes nothing for that record.
- `plan:142` says such differences must be recorded as an explicit policy change.
- Fix:
  - Decide explicitly. I recommend declaring the new behaviour and an oracle expectation: a record's prepared result is not retained if its own filter node fails, and results are interleaved per record.
  - Remove the "preserves the legacy lifecycle" claim.
  - Mark `test_preparation_intake_plan_acceptance.py` as a ported test with a changed expectation. It monkeypatches `PreparationHandler._filter_all`, so it cannot run unchanged against the new controller anyway.

**B3. The maintainability gate cannot be reproduced and can be gamed (P1#9).**
